# Why 91500 comes out ~40 Ma old in Studio (2026-11-05)

Sessions: Boise zircon (4 November 2026, spot files merged with the Rich
CSV), Zhang1 and Zhang2 (Rich CSVs, 232 and 324 acquisitions). All three were
reduced headlessly with the zircon preset: automatic windows, S513 primary
for 206/238, 207/206 and 208/232, NIST610/612 for concentrations, mean
downhole model, quadratic session model, and the arithmetic mean of sweep
ratios. Accepted dates are system-specific and derived from the reference
library's radiogenic ratios. For 91500 (Wiedenbeck 1995) they are 1062.4 Ma
(206/238), 1063.5 Ma (207/235) and 1065.7 Ma (207/206). For S513 (Luo et
al. 2025) they are 561.2, 561.7 and 563.6 Ma.

## Short answer

This is a property of the data and of how Studio chooses the date it
reports. It is not a bug in the corrections or a wrong reference value.

1. Studio's secondary-standard summary uses each spot's preferred date. For
   91500 that date is 207Pb/206Pb, because its 206Pb/238U date is above the
   1000 Ma age transition. 91500 has about 70 ug/g U, so 207Pb runs at only
   about 1500-1900 cps. Each 207/206 date then carries 80-150 Ma (2s),
   against 30-45 Ma for its 206/238 date.
2. The 91500 206/238 dates are accurate within uncertainty in all three
   sessions:

   | session | 91500 206/238 weighted mean (2s) | offset from 1062.4 Ma | 207/206 weighted mean (2s) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Boise zircon | 1056.7 +/- 13.0 Ma | -0.5 % | 1097.3 +/- 33.5 Ma |
   | Zhang1 | 1075.7 +/- 15.7 Ma | +1.2 % | 1103.3 +/- 42.9 Ma |
   | Zhang2 | 1077.7 +/- 14.3 Ma | +1.4 % | 1115.3 +/- 38.6 Ma |

   The UPbR26plus spreadsheet reports 91500 by 206/238 (1050.8 Ma on
   Boise zircon), which is why the spreadsheet looked right and Studio did
   not.
3. The 91500 207/206 ratio really is high, by 1.0-1.7 % in ratio (about 25-50
   Ma in date) in every session. The bias is already in the baseline-subtracted
   counts, before any correction:

   | 207/206 normalized to S513, 91500 | Boise zircon | Zhang1 | Zhang2 |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | ratio of plateau sums | +1.48 % | +1.09 % | +1.68 % |
   | mean of sweep ratios (Studio) | +1.56 % | +0.98 % | +1.65 % |

   Mark Schmitz's spreadsheet (Table S3) shows the same sign on
   Boise zircon but a smaller offset: 91500 207/206 is +0.66 % in ratio,
   with a mean 207/206 date of 1079 Ma. Per spot, UPbRPy's corrected 91500
   207/206 is 0.86 +/- 0.49 % (1s, n 10) higher than the spreadsheet's. For
   S513 the difference is -0.10 %. That difference is 1.8 sigma; it may come
   from the automatic windows and the background estimate, and it is not
   resolved here.

## What was ruled out

- **S513 207/206 anchor.** The TOML stores the Luo et al. (2025) TIMS ratios
  206/238 0.090955 and 207/235 0.73873. The derived 207/206 is 0.058932
  (563.6 Ma). The spreadsheet's mean S513 ratio equals this to 0.003 %, so
  both reductions use the same anchor. LTSE and PL 207/206 ratios agree with
  their accepted values within +/- 0.8 % in every session, so the anchor is
  good to about 0.5 %. The archive-table quirk (rows labelled 206Pb/207Pb i
  that hold 207Pb/204Pb) only affects the common-Pb initial ratios. The S513
  TOML already stores those under the correct keys.
- **Common-Pb correction (204Pb versus 204Hg).** None is applied to the
  preferred 207/206 date (method "207Pb/206Pb"). After the Hg correction,
  91500's mass-204 residual is 0.5-3 cps, so 206Pb/204Pb is undefined for
  most spots. Common Pb therefore cannot be measured here. It also cannot be
  ruled out: f206 = 0.13 % of Stacey-Kramers Pb (207/206 = 0.914) would
  raise 91500's 207/206 by 1.5 %, and that is only about 1.5 cps of 204Pb.
- **Downhole and session corrections for 207/206.** Both are multiplicative
  factors shared by every spot. The mean downhole factor is 1.003-1.008 and
  the session factor is 1.000 +/- 0.002, the same for 91500 and S513. The
  bias is present before either is applied (table above).
- **Detector cross-calibration.** 206Pb and 207Pb were in pulse counting on
  100 % of plateau sweeps for every standard spot in all three sessions. This
  comes from the Rich file's Counter rows: no -33 sentinel on 206 or 207.
  238U was analog on 1 % of S513 sweeps and 3-5 % of PL sweeps, and never
  on 91500.
- **Dead time.** A count-rate effect large enough to move 91500 by +1.5 %
  relative to S513 (206 at 20-25 kcps against 120-155 kcps) would need a
  dead-time error of about 125 ns, several times the dead time itself.
- **Mean of sweep ratios (Jensen bias).** Mean of ratios over ratio of sums
  is +0.2 % for 91500 and +0.1-0.3 % for S513, so nearly all of it cancels.
- **Automatic windows on 91500's low signal.** The signals are about 9 s
  long with 18 plateau sweeps, and the windows are the same shape as on
  S513. Binned by time since signal start, the 91500 excess over S513 is
  not concentrated in the first second. It is spread over the ablation,
  which does not look like surface common Pb.

## "Reverse fractionation" flag: a bug, fixed

The flag fired on 6/8, 4/8 and 5/10 91500 spots, but also on 10/22, 12/28
and 22/38 S513 spots. It is the workbook's diagnostic for a raw 206Pb/238U
ratio that falls during ablation. UPbRPy took the slope of the
fractionation-corrected ratio, which is noise around zero, so the flag fired
spuriously across the sessions. Taken from the raw ratio, it fires on no
reference zircon. Fixed in this commit series: `calculate_spot_quality_metrics` takes
`slope_ratio_column`, and `finalize_reduction_results` passes the raw
`206Pb/238U`. A regression test is in
`tests/test_reverse_fractionation_flag.py`. The flag never affected dates.

## Bias against composition and signal (all 196 standard spots)

Weighted straight-line fits of the relative date offset (%) against log10 of
each quantity, pooled over S513, 91500, Seiland, PL and LTSE and all three
sessions. Slopes are in percent per decade, 2s, MSWD-inflated.

| x | 206/238 slope | p | 207/206 slope | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| U | -0.08 +/- 0.60 | 0.80 | -3.6 +/- 2.1 | 0.001 |
| Pb (208-based) | +0.02 +/- 1.63 | 0.98 | -11.7 +/- 6.8 | 0.001 |
| total REE | -0.65 +/- 1.22 | 0.29 | +9.3 +/- 6.7 | 0.006 |
| 206Pb cps | -0.02 +/- 0.70 | 0.96 | -5.1 +/- 2.9 | 0.001 |
| 238U cps | +0.16 +/- 0.59 | 0.60 | -3.5 +/- 2.1 | 0.001 |

- **206/238:** no trend with U, Pb, REE or signal. The weak per-session
  trends on Boise zircon (REE p 0.008, Pb p 0.03) reverse sign on Zhang1
  and Zhang2.
- **207/206:** offsets grow as the signal falls. U, Pb, REE and cps are
  collinear across these materials: 91500 and Seiland are low-U and
  high-REE. One spot set cannot separate them, and 91500 carries the trend.
  Seiland, with the weakest signal (400-550 cps 207Pb), does not follow a
  simple 1/signal common-Pb model. Its 207/206 dates scatter by tens of
  percent and its ratio-of-sums offsets are +1.8, -1.3 and -4.6 %.

The secondaries' 206/238 offsets also shift together by session: about -0.5
to -2.2 % on Boise zircon and +0.5 to +1.4 % on Zhang1 and Zhang2, each
within its 2s. That is a session-level S513 normalization effect, not a
composition effect.

## What Studio should do (proposals, not implemented)

1. **Judge secondary standards per date system against system-specific
   accepted dates.** Always show 206/238 against the material's 206/238 date
   (1062.4 Ma for 91500, not 1065.4). Show 207/206 against its 207/206 date
   only when the 207/206 date is the more precise one. Today the summary
   compares the preferred date with `concordia_age`.
2. **Make the 206/238-to-207/206 switch precision-aware.** Prefer 207/206
   only above the transition and when its 2s is smaller than the 206/238 2s,
   or move the default transition to 1500 Ma. Either way, 91500 in these
   sessions is reported by 206/238.
3. **Report Pb properly.** Studio's Pb concentration is calibrated from
   208Pb against NIST natural Pb. For zircon it reads 2.4 ug/g for 91500,
   against the spreadsheet's 14.9 ug/g total Pb. Add a total or radiogenic
   Pb from 206+207+208.
4. Use the new Bias page (206/238 and 207/206 against U, cps and REE) as the
   routine check. A falling 207/206 trend with signal is the signature
   to watch for undetected common Pb on low-U standards.

Working files (not in the repository): the per-spot table with dates,
offsets, concentrations, 206/204, cps, detector modes and correction factors
was written to `standards_table.csv` in the investigating session's
scratchpad.
